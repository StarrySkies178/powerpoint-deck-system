The decks don't share one brand. They do share one way of building slides, the consulting style. That shared way of working is what goes into your style. The other firms' logos and exact brand colours stay out.

## The shared rules (your house style)

**Writing (from BCG, followed by all the others)**
1. **The title states the conclusion**: a full sentence, at most 2 lines ("Profits growing faster than sales", not "Profits").
2. **One message per slide.** The body proves the title and nothing else.
3. An optional **subtitle or unit line** under the title (e.g. "US$ m, 2020–24").
4. An optional **takeaway box** at the bottom for the "so what".
5. **Source and footnotes** at bottom left on every data slide. Page number at bottom right.
6. Choose the format by what you're showing:
   - change over time → column or line chart
   - parts of a whole → stacked or 100% chart
   - ranking or comparison → bar chart
   - correlation → scatter or bubble chart
   - spatial → map
   - steps → process or flow
   - qualitative ideas → word slide or conceptual graphic

**Layout**
7. The title sits top-left in **the same place on every slide**, with a thin rule or clear space below it. Content goes in one body area, and the footer zone holds the source, page number and logo.
8. Use a grid. Boxes in a row are the same size, and the gaps between them are equal.
9. Use flat 2D shapes. The newest decks (Deloitte, EY) have dropped the 3D and gradients of the 2002–2008 ones.

**Colour**
10. **One main colour plus a set of greys** does most of the work. Grey is for context, and colour marks the point the slide is making.
11. **One accent, used sparingly.** EY's rule is to use yellow only to highlight the key point. Every deck follows the same idea.
12. Charts: data labels on, few or no gridlines, grey for "others", and the main colour for the series that matters.

**Type**
13. One sans-serif family. Arial is used in 9 of the 11 decks.
14. Sizes: titles 20–30 pt, subtitles 14–18 pt bold, body 12–16 pt, chart labels 10–11 pt, footnotes 8–9 pt.
15. For Chinese text, the decks pair Arial with 宋体 / 微软雅黑 (Microsoft YaHei).

## Object vocabulary (what goes in Objects/)

- **Text:** numbered points, callout boxes, takeaway boxes, labels, tags, Harvey balls
- **Data:** column, bar, stacked and 100% charts, waterfall, line, scatter/bubble, Mekko, pie/doughnut, KPI number, data table
- **Flow:** chevrons, process steps, timelines, Gantt charts, swimlanes, circular flows and cycles, arrows, funnels, consequences (if → then)
- **Structure:** 2×2 matrix, pyramid, onion/target, Venn, honeycomb, logic/issue tree, org chart, value chain, frameworks (5 forces, 7S)
- **Visual:** balance/lever, gears, ladder, puzzle, plus/minus, maps (world, US, China and its provinces)

## Decisions still open
