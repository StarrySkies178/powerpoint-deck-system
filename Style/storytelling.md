# Storytelling rules — how the 11 template decks tell a story

These rules come from reading every slide in the 11 decks. Where a deck states a rule itself, its source is given in brackets.

## 1. Plan the deck before the slides

1. **Write the storyline first, as an executive summary tree** [Deloitte 40-41]. Start with one main conclusion, add 3–5 chapter messages that prove it, then the supporting points under each chapter. Every slide title in the deck comes from this tree.
2. **Start with the audience's concerns.** Tell their story, not your process, and keep the same key characters and concepts all the way through [BCG 46].
3. **Size the detail to the audience** [BCG 15]:

   | Audience | Detail | Length | Style |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Working team | High | 2–4 h | Discussion |
   | Business managers | As needed | 1–2 h | Q&A |
   | Board / executives | Low | 20–30 min | Formal; few words, big visuals |

4. **Decide whether it's a presentation or a leave-behind.** Slides presented live should be simple, because the presenter carries the story. A leave-behind has to stand on its own, so add a takeaway box, notes and sources [BCG 12-14].
5. **Pick a deck structure that fits the job:**
   - **Recommendation (pyramid):** main message → executive summary → one section per chapter → next steps.
   - **Problem-solving (SCR):** situation → complication → resolution → plan.
   - **Proposal / credentials** [EY 220-245]: cover → what's changing in your world → the questions you face → how we're set up to help (3 pillars) → one slide per pillar → delivery model → global reach in big numbers → divider "success stories" → case studies (challenge / what we did / result) → recognition → thought leadership → call to action.
   - **Case study** [EY 199-200, ATK 165, QPT 273]: client background → business challenge → how we helped → value delivered (highlighted).
   - **Workplan** [McK, QPT tools]: objective → approach (phases) → workplan / Gantt → team and governance → next steps.

## 2. Every slide has the same anatomy [BCG 47]

```
TITLE      = the main point (a full sentence; the conclusion, not the topic)
subtitle   = what the exhibit shows + unit  ("Revenue by segment, US$ m, 2019-24")
BODY       = proof of the title: words, table, schematic or chart
TAKEAWAY   = the "so what", or the bridge to the next slide (box/bar at bottom; may be spoken)
notes/source (bottom left)          page number / tracker (bottom right / top right)
```

- **Title formula:** context at the start, new information at the end [BCG 48]. At most 2 lines, no full stop at the end.
  - Write "Online share doubled to 34% in three years", not "Online share".
- **One message per slide.** Six key points at most [BCG 55]. One message is not one figure and not few words: the case decks prove the message with 2 to 4 exhibits and read each one in 2 to 4 bullets (§8 rules 11 and 17).
- **The body proves the title and nothing else.** If something doesn't support the title, delete it or move it to the appendix.
- **Keep the body parallel:** the same subject and the same grammatical form for each point [BCG 48].
- **The takeaway bar** is the slide's exclamation point [BCG 49]. A.T. Kearney calls it the "tombstone": a full-width coloured bar with bold 18 pt text.
- **Sources go on every data slide** [BCG 55, ATK, McK].
- **Status stickers:** use ILLUSTRATIVE / PRELIMINARY / DRAFT / BACKUP in upper case, top right [McK 156, RB 89].

## 3. Choose the format from the message [BCG 17-35]

**First ask: what do you want the audience to take away?**

- To remember a trend or picture a flow → **graphic**
- To refer to specific numbers or see how they're calculated → **table** (usually in the appendix)
- To follow a logic flow or a recommendation → **word slide** or **conceptual graphic**
- Consider a graphic first. If a graphic doesn't work, use words or a table.
- Combining a graphic with words (for example a chart plus customer quotes) makes the message stronger [BCG 35-36].

**Data: match the chart to the relationship** [BCG 23-32, ATK]

| You want to show | Use | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Change over time, ≤10 periods | Column | Time runs left to right; label values; show CAGR |
| Change over time, >10 points, or the slope is the point | Line / area | Label line ends instead of using a legend |
| Ranking or comparing items | Bar (horizontal) | Sort by value; room for long labels; highlight the client |
| Parts of a whole / mix | Stacked or 100% column | Totals above; series lines between stacks show the shift |
| One total bridging to another | Waterfall | Start and end bars in the accent colour |
| Size and share together | Mekko | Width = segment size |
| Share at one point in time, ≤5 slices | Pie / doughnut | One slice in the accent colour, others grey; break it out to a stacked column for detail |
| Relationship between variables | Scatter | Trend line only if you're sure of the point |
| Third dimension | Bubble | Size must be a size variable (assets, sales) |
| Many dimensions vs a benchmark | Radar | Two series at most |
| Units and % together | Column + line, dual axis | Only for units vs %; nothing else |
| Spatial differences | Map | Grey base, focus regions in the accent colour |
| Same measure for several entities | Small multiples | Shared scale; at most 3 stacked vertically [ATK 69] |

**Concepts: match the graphic to the story.** The full list is in pattern-catalogue.md.

| The story is… | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Steps in order | chevron-process, staircase, timeline |
| A loop that repeats | cycle, virtuous-cycle, feedback-loop |
| Parts that make a whole | hub-spoke, honeycomb, wedges, temple, puzzle |
| Layers / hierarchy | pyramid, target-onion |
| Narrowing / selection | funnel, filter |
| Two dimensions decide | matrix-2x2, bubble-portfolio |
| Two sides | pros-cons, balance, before-after, opposing |
| Pressures on something | forces-in |
| Effects of something | impact-out, chain-reaction |
| Many things, one goal | converge, synthesis |
| One thing, many paths | options-branch |
| Something in the way | barrier |
| A problem broken down | issue-tree, driver-tree |

## 4. Emphasis: one point per slide

This is the strongest rule across all 11 decks:

- **Grey does the work, colour makes the point.**
  - Bain: grey shapes with one red element.
  - EY: "use yellow in a controlled way… to highlight… or draw attention to an important piece of information. Keep usage of yellow to a minimum."
  - Deloitte: "Gray can be used as a base color, with any of the Deloitte colors used to pull out key points."
  - A.T. Kearney: highlight one series in red, the rest olive or grey.
- **Highlight what the title says.** If the title is about step 3, step 3 is the coloured one.
- **Use tints only after the solid colours are used up** [Deloitte 260].
- **Body text is dark grey, not black and not coloured** [Deloitte 260].

## 5. Visual discipline: lean, linear, literal [Roland Berger 89-90]

- No shading or borders on boxes. Use unframed shapes in shades of one key colour, and use colour only for emphasis.
- No ovals. Use fully rounded rectangles.
- Put numbers in squares (key colour, white bold), not circles.
- No big block arrows. Use thinner arrow and bracket variations.
- No speech bubbles for content. Use callouts.
- No clip art. Use monochrome line pictograms.
- Never put text behind a graphic. Put it beside the graphic.
- Navigators are numbered squares, not mini charts.
- Avoid long paragraphs, keep bullets concise, and use no distracting transitions [Deloitte 254].
- Chart text [ATK 94]:
  - Axes visible, with 4–5 tick labels at most.
  - Tick labels 10 pt; series labels 12 pt bold.
  - Data labels always on; totals bold.
  - No legend if labels can sit on the chart [QPT 331].

## 6. Variety comes from the content

- Sort each slide by its message type first (section 3). The pattern follows from that, so different messages get different slides.
- Don't use the same pattern on two slides in a row. Alternate concept slides, data slides and text slides.
- For the same pattern, use a variant (horizontal/vertical, ring/radial, highlighted segment, with/without callouts).
- Section dividers (full colour) and trackers (current section highlighted) give a long deck rhythm [Bain, Deloitte, QPT 327].

## 7. Before calling a slide done [BCG 54-55]

1. Does the slide contribute to the story?
2. Does it address what the audience needs?
3. Is the point clear in the title?
4. Is the title linked to the body?
5. Is the body parallel and easy to read?
6. Is there unneeded material?
7. Does the data have a source?
8. Is the format and colour consistent?
9. No typos.

## 8. Storyline rules from the case-competition study (all presentations) [Style/refs/case-competitions.md]

1. **Answer first.** Open with a one-page executive summary in one of four forms: lettered conclusions (A/B/C, letters reused in section titles), decision framework (stages → what we analysed → answer → goal bar), story diagram (findings → target → strategies → next steps) or pyramid (governing thought → key lines → supports). Generators: `letteredSummary`, `decisionFramework`, `storyDiagram`, `pyramidSummary`.
2. **Title chain test.** Read the titles alone before building: they must read as one argument. Use connectives where a slide follows from the one before: *therefore, hence, meanwhile, furthermore, then, in the long term*.
3. **Evidence → decision.** Close every block of evidence with a decision slide ("Therefore, we…") that shows the choice as a table or matrix.
4. **Problem before solution.** In a recommendation, put one problem slide before each strategy pillar.
5. **Role tags in long decks** (> 12 slides): "Analysis n:", "Strategy n:", "S-term:", "L-term:", the value-chain stage, or the exec-summary letter (A, B, C1…).
6. **Fixed so-what.** The so-what of an analysis slide sits in the same place on every slide (bottom-right box `soWhat()` or the takeaway bar).
7. **Panels.** At most 3 numbered panels per slide; each sub-headline is a sentence; together they prove the title (`evidencePanels`, `subhead`).
8. **Value bridge.** A deck with financials shows a waterfall from today's value to the target, by initiative (`waterfall`).
9. **Recurring characters.** Name customer personas and key competitors once and reuse them, with the same name, picture and colour, on every later slide.
10. **Message → figure.** Choose the exhibit from the message using the table in `Style/refs/case-competitions.md` §5, and make the point with one highlight (circle, box or accent colour), never with a second colour scheme.
11. **Several figures per slide (hard rule since v2.10; the user: "one figure leaves a lot of white space; have multiple figures and infographics, intuitive").** An analysis slide carries 2–4 exhibits that together prove its title (78% of the case-deck content slides do; their median slide fills 93–98% of its body, measured with `scripts/deck_lint.py` on 11 decks, 197 slides). A single exhibit is the exception: at most one content slide in four. An empty-looking slide is missing proof, so add evidence, not size. And the evidence takes a form the references use: v2.10 filled the summary with grey KPI tiles ('the numbers behind the answer'), which no reference deck has; the user caught it ("do the reference slides do this? I don't think so"). The one reference summary with numbers (Baincraft 2) has a plain Projection column: header bar, label, big number, then Risks. Pick the layout from `Style/refs/case-competitions.md` §4b (HALF2, GRID4I, BAND2, T2B1/T1B2, GRID4, EXI, L2R1, COL3, FLOW, CHAIN, EXB) and build it with `composeSlide()`. Rules: every panel numbered with a sentence sub-headline; Z reading order with the conclusion (insights / so-what) in the last cell; same palette, categories and highlight across panels, all pointing to the same answer; connectors only when one panel follows from another; at most 4 panels, otherwise a table or the appendix; gutters, not boxes.
12. **Key questions before the strategy.** Between the analysis and the recommendation, put one slide that checks each finding against the client's capability (✓ strength / ✗ gap) and turns it into a numbered key question; the strategy section answers those questions by number (2019–20 study §3).
13. **Name the strategy and letter its pillars.** Give the strategy a short name or mission line and reuse it in the executive summary, the strategy overview and the close; letter the pillars (A/B/C, sub-steps A1, A2) and put the letter chip before every title of that pillar.
14. **Risks as a numbered heat map.** Show risks as numbered dots on likelihood × impact with the priority zone marked, and use the same numbers in the risk → mitigation list beside it (`riskMatrix`).
15. **Show how the target is built, then phase it.** A headline target (stores, revenue, users) is shown as a value tree whose leaves are tied to the lettered initiatives (`valueTree`); the roadmap is a Gantt grouped by pillar under named phases (`gantt` with `phases`). New exec-summary form: situation → pillars → outcome (`composeSlide` layout `T3B1`). New layouts: T3B1, COL4/COLN, FLOW with 2–4 blocks, a takeaway box under each panel (`panel.takeaway`). See `Style/refs/case-competitions-2019-2020.md`.
16. **Decompose a place from the whole to the part.** When the answer is "where", go level by level (country → region → city → district → site → building → floor → zone) and stop at the level the decision is made. At each level the chosen part is in the main colour and the rest grey, and the next level zooms into exactly that part (zoom lines, the same highlight or a breadcrumb). Put the selection logic (criteria, score, formula or funnel) next to the map, and give every pin or zone its numbers in a callout. Builders: `mapRegions`, `regionMultiples`, `geoZoom`, `siteCallouts`, `buildingExplode`, `floorPlan` in `Generators/patterns_place.js`; map data in `Objects/maps/` (more with `scripts/get_map.py`). Name the map source in the source line. See `Style/refs/case-competitions-2019-2020.md` §10.
17. **Read every exhibit on the slide (since v2.16; self-audit 2026-11-23).** The reference decks carry 140 to 211 body words on their median content slide, because each figure sits with its unit line and 2 to 4 bullets that say what it shows, key words bold in the main colour, in a grey box under or beside it (Mindcraft financial slides, Baincraft 5, E-Cluster 1). A figure with one line under it is unfinished. `reading:` and `unit:` on a panel; the lint warns under 90 words and over 260.
18. **Title block as the references have it.** Section breadcrumb above the title (current section bold, main colour), a full two-line action title, no grey subtitle line. The letter of the executive summary lives in the breadcrumb and the agenda, not typed into the title.
19. **Annotated charts and mixed forms.** A trend carries its growth rate, a comparison its growth bubbles, a stack its CAGR table, and one series or point is highlighted. Charts alternate with tables, diagrams, maps and matrices; evaluation uses Harvey balls or ticks with a legend. **No form more than twice in a deck, never on neighbouring slides** (since v2.18): the v0.5 sample put a Harvey-ball scoring table on three of four neighbouring slides, and the user asked "you think you do this too many times?". The references use it once or twice per deck, for the decision between options; a market comparison is a positioning map, a shortlist a table of facts.
20. **Grey boxes and colour bars, not accent-bar cards.** Readings and takeaways are grey filled boxes; the so-what is a full-colour bar. A white card with a coloured bar on its left edge is not in any reference.
21. **Pictures with a job.** A line icon marks each panel head or label point; the cover and the agenda carry one full-bleed photo with a colour panel. Photos and icons follow `Objects/images.md` (free licence, original resolution, credit logged).
