# How to judge a slide build the way Mike does

You will see one picture with two slides side by side: the ORIGINAL from a real consulting deck on the LEFT, and a BUILD an AI system made from a text brief on the RIGHT. Say whether Mike, the owner, would accept the build next to the original.

Mike's goal right now is mimicry: "bare minimum is you should be able to mimic". He does not want a pixel copy, and he does not audit every cell. He asks one thing: does the build give the same overall impression as the original, as a complete, professionally made slide? When he rejects builds, his words are: the originals "look more professional", the builds "look AI and not professional", and "sometimes there are white blank gaps".

## How to look

1. Look at both halves at full size, then zoom into each region of the build (title, every chart, table or box, the footer) and compare it with the same region of the original. Zoom to confirm what you see, not to hunt for tiny faults.
2. Then step back and decide on the whole slide. Seen side by side at normal size, does the original look clearly better made, or does the build look like a plainer, emptier or broken slide? Only faults that change that overall impression make a build NOT OK.

## Slide-level faults (these make a build NOT OK)

A fault is slide-level when it is in the title or the main visual, when it repeats across the slide, when it covers a large area (roughly a quarter of the slide or a whole column), or when several different faults appear together.

**Design dropped and not replaced.** The original has a clear design treatment (a dark or coloured background, shaded panels, boxes, cards, a filled header band), and the build drops it and puts nothing designed in its place, leaving plain default text on white. A slide that has become a bare page of paragraphs is NOT OK even if every word is there.

**White blank gaps.** A large empty area where the original has content: a whole band, a column half empty, the bottom third blank, charts floating in white space.

**Careless text across the slide.**
- Body text much smaller than the original's, everywhere, so the slide reads as tiny print in a big frame.
- Bullet lists flattened into run-on sentences, or the heading and sub-point hierarchy lost throughout.
- Repeated glitches such as doubled bullet marks or mixed font sizes inside lists.
- The title or a header band clipped, cut off, or jammed against an edge with no padding.

**Charts stripped or broken.** The main chart loses its parts (axis, connector lines, total labels, a side panel of figures, subtitles, legend), its colours become flat default greys, or its labels overlap so badly that most of them cannot be read.

**Structure lost.** The original's building blocks (takeaway boxes, numbered section headers, connecting arrows, row labels) are dropped or replaced by plain text, or the content is poured into a generic grid the original does not have.

**Main visual dumbed down.** The visual that carries the message (a heat map, a matrix with items placed on it, a timeline, a flow) is redrawn as a plainer, schematic version: fewer shades, items grouped or moved to a legend instead of placed on it, a highlight box no longer framing the right area. This is NOT OK even if the rest is clean; Mike's words were "could be better".

## Local flaws (tolerate these when the rest is complete and well made)

- One empty cell or one short gap in an otherwise full layout.
- One list or text block running slightly past its box, or text set tight to a cell edge.
- A few crowded, close or slightly misplaced data labels in a chart that keeps all its parts.
- Wrong values, wrong bar lengths, or some rows of a chart without their bars. Mike is judging the look, not checking the data.
- Different colours or background, when the build uses its own equally designed treatment instead (numbered cards, shaded boxes, coloured emphasis).
- Restyling the same structure: filled shapes instead of outlined ones, a shaded table instead of grid lines, arrows instead of plain bars, a paler header fill, swapped series colours.
- A different chart form of equal richness (for example a pie for a 100% stacked bar, all parts labelled).
- Loose placement, different spacing, missing logos, a different footer or page number.

Builds Mike accepted had one or two such flaws. Do not fail a complete, polished build because zooming in found a small imperfection.

## Where the line is

OK: an imperfect but professional mimic. Same message and main parts, space used about as fully as the original, text readable, main visual as rich as the original's, and a designed look (the original's or an equally good one of its own). Small local flaws are fine.

NOT OK: at least one slide-level fault, so that next to the original the build looks plainer, emptier, broken or generic. Faithful layout does not save a plain, undesigned build; a different style does not sink a polished, complete one.

If you are unsure whether a fault is local or slide-level, ask whether Mike would notice it within a few seconds at normal size without zooming. If not, it is local.

## How to answer

Answer in exactly two lines and nothing else.

Line 1: `OK` or `NOT OK`
Line 2: one short reason in plain words naming the main thing you saw (for example: "design dropped, left as plain paragraphs on white" or "complete, polished mimic; one empty cell and restyled colours are minor").
